# Discretization Safety Certification

## Certified Claim

For every parsed SysML `#Prohibition` and `#Obligation`, the stage checks that
the complete modeled physical process satisfies the requirement at a
controller update and at every time through the following fixed `dt` interval.
The executed action must satisfy the `#NeuralRequirement` and is held through
the interval according to the extracted actuator and physical equations.

Certification concerns the physical system rather than only the values visible
to the controller. Unobserved physical state and every equation required by the
property remain part of the proof obligation.

In this document, an annotated `#Obligation` is one class of SysML system
requirement. A proof obligation is any constraint generated by the
certification process, including constraints derived from either
`#Prohibition` or `#Obligation`.

A case is a factored unsafe proof unit for either the update point or the
physical interval. It can remain a factored Boolean root instead of being
expanded into every branch.

## Checked Inputs and Timing

The stage accepts the SysML model, its Markov process certificate, its reduced
MDP specification, and the run-wide `dt`. Before analysis it checks that the
Markov claim is discharged, both preprocessing artifacts pass their own
checkers, and their model paths, source hashes, and time steps match the current
request.

The Markov certificate supplies the reconstructed state, executed action
history, shield interface, and sampled transition evidence. Observation and
action buffers represent information needed to reconstruct the sampled state.
They are not converted into elapsed physical time. Physical trajectories use
one interval variable ranging from zero through the fixed `dt`.

## Physical Interval Reduction

The reducer reparses the SysML source and constructs one obligation for each
annotated system requirement. It performs the following operations.

1. Expand definitions evaluated during the same controller update.
2. Map controller readings to physical quantities through the extracted sensor
   equations and guards.
3. Reconstruct the controller contract and map its outputs to executed action
   variables through the actuator equations.
4. Apply scenario bounds, initial relations, policy call guards, declared
   constants, and variable types.
5. Interpret each relevant `#ContinuousRate` update as a physical trajectory
   over the interval while the selected action is held.
6. Check that each trajectory endpoint equals the sampled next state extracted
   from the SysML transition.
7. Construct the unsafe formulas for the update point and the complete physical
   interval.
8. Construct the sampled transition and initial state relation used by
   reachability methods.

Operationally, the reducer substitutes an interval variable `t` for `dt` in an
annotated sampled update after expanding the controller and actuator values
effective for the interval. Thus `x := x + r * dt` becomes
`x(t) = x + r * t` for `0 <= t <= dt`, with `r` evaluated from update point
quantities and the executed action held fixed. The stage does not solve a
general differential equation.

The certificate records the complete equation inventory and the dependency
closure selected for each property. An equation cannot be omitted because its
state is absent from the neural observation.

## Lazy Constraint Construction

Unsafe conditions remain factored instead of being expanded into every Boolean
and conditional combination. A checker requests only the branches or
conjunctive subset needed for its proof. The analysis records the selected
constraints and evidence that they cover the original obligation.

Canonical expression hashes identify identical subsets across cases. Within a
grouped analysis, an identical subset is checked once, certified subsets are
minimized when possible, and the result is reused for every covered case.
Reachability uses a separate shared context keyed by the complete domain,
initial relation, transition relation, action variables, and numeric types.
These choices prevent many duplicated obligations from being created or solved
while preserving explicit coverage.

## Checker Progression

Every unresolved case follows the same progression.

```text
linear
convex
reachability_linear
reachability_convex
exact_symbolic
smt_fallback
relational_invariant
smt_reachability
```

Linear and convex methods are attempted first because they produce small proof
objects that can be replayed exactly. Symbolic, invariant, and SMT methods are
used only when the simpler reductions defer.

The linear checker accepts only exact linear reductions and records
nonnegative rational multipliers whose weighted constraints produce a
contradiction. The convex checker accepts its supported quadratic forms and
records exact rational evidence for the resulting global bound. Numerical
optimizer output is not accepted by itself as a certificate.

The symbolic and local SMT methods handle logical structure not discharged by
the arithmetic checkers. Reachability methods distinguish a locally feasible
unsafe condition from behavior reachable from the declared initial state and
complete sampled transition. A local satisfying assignment is therefore not a
physical counterexample. `VIOLATION` requires a reachable trace accepted by
exact replay.

Each checker attempt has one of three outcomes.

| outcome | meaning |
|---|---|
| `CERTIFIED` | the recorded analysis claims that proof evidence establishes the case |
| `VIOLATION` | a reachable counterexample passes exact replay |
| `DEFERRED` | applicability, proof, or replay was not established |

Unsupported expressions, missing equations, malformed evidence, numerical
failure, solver failure, and timeout produce `DEFERRED`. `NOT_CERTIFIED` means
that model-wide certification was not completed and no replayed violation was
established. It is not a report that unsafe behavior occurred.

## Certificate Boundary

Certificate generation and checking use separate modules. The certificate binds
the SysML source, Markov certificate, reduced specification, fixed `dt`,
reduction records, checker attempts, and proof evidence through canonical
hashes. Repeated proof subtrees are stored once in a content-addressed pool to
reduce certificate size.

The checker does not call the analysis generator. It validates the input
artifacts and hashes, reparses the SysML source, reconstructs the controller
contract and physical reduction, expands the compact proof records, and checks
supported proof evidence recorded in the checker attempts. It does not
independently recompute the top level, property, or case result aggregation, and
it does not require a nonempty checker progression for every recorded case.
